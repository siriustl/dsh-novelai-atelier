# NovelAI V4.5: concise tags and English scene descriptions

> Evidence provenance: documented behavior and frontend snapshots originate in prior historical material. Public model/prompting documentation was rechecked on 2026-10-02. Current tool schemas determine supported fields and model combinations; historical snapshots do not prove current deployment.

Applies to `nai-diffusion-4-5-full` and `nai-diffusion-4-5-curated`. V4.5 remains useful when the user likes its rendering, needs a V4.5-only feature, or wants Opus generation without V5's rechargeable allowance. A model change still changes the image and should be visible to the user.

## What carries over from V5

Both families support tags plus natural language, a base scene prompt plus separate character prompts, per-character UC, source/target/mutual action tags, numerical emphasis, and negative numerical emphasis. Both use the `fur dataset` prefix for furry work and `background dataset` for photographic images without people. Both are anime/illustration models by default; the photographic dataset prefix is an optional branch for a requested photographic scene, not the normal background-art workflow.

Use concise lowercase tags and normal English grammar/capitalization in prose. Select the subject count and gender from the user's request, not from a copied template. `1boy, solo` or `1girl, solo` establishes a male or female subject when requested. Do not mechanically append an adulthood phrase or numerical age. For objects, scenery, and creatures do not inject human count tags or human anatomy.

## What must change when adapting V5 prompts

| Feature | V4.5 rule |
|---|---|
| Context | Approximately 512 T5 tokens across **base and all character prompts together** |
| Language | Prefer English; the T5 tokenizer does not support most Unicode such as Japanese characters and colorful emoji |
| Characters | Up to 6 separate characters |
| Positions | 5×5 grid; align explicit coordinates to supported grid positions |
| Lettering | English, up to 118 characters including spaces/newlines |
| V5-only tags | Do not assume V5-specific trained meanings for `attractive male`, `depthness`, complexity or era tags; ordinary descriptions may still be understood |
| Alpha | Do not promise native transparent-background generation |
| Free Opus | Ordinary eligible single-image generations have no V5 usage bucket |
| Precise Reference | Available for V4.5, adds Anlas; select the reference workflow and inspect its live schema |
| Vibe Transfer | Available for V4.5; compatible encoded vibes can be reused, but not with Precise Reference or inpainting |

There is no mandatory V4.5 substitute for `attractive male`. Specify the desired anime character design with suitable hairstyle, eyes, expression, silhouette and costume. Do not compensate for a missing V5-specific control by automatically adding realistic bone structure, age or facial-hair descriptors.

## Spend the context budget on visible information

Use a hierarchy:

1. Count, framing, and identity.
2. One understandable action or pose.
3. Distinctive face/hair/costume anchors.
4. Setting, lighting, and a coherent medium/palette.
5. Natural language where useful for the relationship, as short or detailed as the scene needs within the budget. This is not a one-sentence restriction; pure prose is also supported.

Remove repeated quality adjectives, mutually exclusive styles, hidden body details, and clothing below the crop. With multiple characters, shared light/style normally goes in the base. Individual style or action can go in its own slot when intended; avoid purposeless duplication.

Example:

```text
no humans, landscape, mountain lake, pine forest, distant mountains, morning mist, watercolor (medium), muted colors. The dark forest forms a narrow band between the pale lake and the mist. A small wooden pier enters from the lower left and points toward the distant mountain pass.
```

The exact token count depends on the tokenizer; a word or character count is only a rough hint. The plugin's quality suffix also consumes space. If the tool cannot tokenize, leave headroom rather than claiming to know the remaining token budget.

## Full versus Curated quality behavior

The current frontend preset snapshot and this plugin compile these standard suffixes:

```text
Full:
very aesthetic, masterpiece, no text

Curated:
very aesthetic, masterpiece, no text, -0.8::feet::, rating:general
```

The official Quality Tags documentation still lists older V4.5 suffixes containing `location`, unlike the inspected frontend and this plugin. Use `nai_get_presets` for the text actually injected here. Curated's negative `feet` weight is worth checking if the result has a foot problem; compare a custom suffix without it when relevant, rather than treating all full-body images as requiring the preset off. `no text` also matters when lettering is requested.

Official Light UC also differs:

```text
Full:
lowres, artistic error, scan artifacts, worst quality, bad quality, jpeg artifacts, multiple views, very displeasing, too many watermarks, negative space, blank page

Curated:
blurry, lowres, upscaled, artistic error, scan artifacts, jpeg artifacts, logo, too many watermarks, negative space, blank page
```

Official Heavy UC for Full additionally suppresses film grain, chromatic aberration, dithering, halftone, and screentone. Curated Heavy uses a slightly different list; do not blindly copy a V5/Full negative preset to Curated and call it the official preset. For intentional manga, texture, sparse composition, or turnaround views, a custom UC is often the more coherent choice.

## Weighting

`{tag}` multiplies weight by 1.05. `{{tag}}` is 1.1025, not an enormous emphasis. `[tag]` divides by 1.05. Explicit `1.2::tag::` is often easier to reason about than a forest of brackets. A plain closing `::` also closes outstanding brackets.

V4.5 supports negative values, e.g. `-1::hat::`; this targeted removal can be more direct than a long UC list. Negative emphasis can also invert concepts (`-1::monochrome::` tends toward color), but inversion is a creative heuristic rather than a logical guarantee. Large negatives may move the whole image into a different visual regime. Do not automatically copy the strongest values from showcase prompts.

A useful iteration is: establish the composition with unweighted tags, then modestly strengthen the one important feature being missed. If the feature remains missing, check conflicting framing, presets, and pose instructions before increasing weight again.

## Multiple characters

```text
Base:
2boys, side-by-side, upper body, workshop, warm light. Two men examine a wooden model on a table. The bearded man on the left points to it; the black-haired man on the right watches.

Character 1:
boy, grey hair, beard, brown eyes, tan skin, brown vest, white shirt

Character 2:
boy, short black hair, blue eyes, pale skin, navy jacket
```

For human characters, count tags belong in the base; individual prompts use `boy` without a number. Choose separate left/right positions on the supported grid if the tool exposes them, and keep list order and prose consistent. If the beard leaks to the second character, add `beard` to **his** UC rather than globally excluding beards and erasing the first man's defining feature.

Object interaction can benefit from plain English. The positioning slots can also be used for animals or objects when useful; do not invent human gender labels for them. Do not invent action tags as if they were guaranteed API commands. The documented `source#`, `target#`, and `mutual#` prefixes are useful for supported relationships, with imperfect reliability.

## English lettering

Include `text, english text`; describe placement in prose; put `Text:` and the literal words at the end. Keep the total lettering at or below 118 characters including blank lines, and omit `no text`. V4.5 does not normally need the all-uppercase workaround suggested for V4.

```text
1boy, solo, upper body, blue jacket, poster, text, english text, simple background. A smiling traveler holds a folded map. The title is printed above his head in large clear letters.
Text: NEXT STOP
```

Use the tool's `text` field when supported so that its compiler can put quality tags in the correct place. Do not append a quality suffix after text or send both a `Text:` block and the separate field.
